# 🤝 Cross-Cultural Community Operations

> Turning audience interests into experiences people actively choose to join.

`Community growth` `Content planning` `Cross-cultural communication` `Project delivery`

## 🎬 Case 1: Korean Society

**Role:** President; led a 16-person team  
**Audience need:** Students wanted accessible cultural and social experiences, not only formal introductions to Korean culture.

### 🛠️ Actions

- Managed activity positioning, programme design, promotion, task allocation, participant communication, and on-site delivery.
- Designed a campus film-screening activity around shared cultural interest and low-barrier social participation.
- Organised a flea market that combined community interaction with practical value.

### 🚀 Results

- All **150 film-screening places** were filled quickly.
- The flea market attracted **500+ participants**.
- The team coordinated promotion, logistics, participant experience, and live problem-solving across the full project cycle.

### 💡 Learning

Content and community operations are closely linked: a strong theme attracts attention, but clear participation design and reliable delivery determine whether interest becomes engagement.

## 🌱 Case 2: Environmental Science student organisation

**Role:** President; led a 20-person team

I helped create original activities from audience-interest assessment through execution. One 40-person activity reached capacity soon after registration opened. The work required balancing educational value with an experience students would actively choose to join.

## 🎨 Case 3: Public-facing environmental content

As activity lead for the *Paths & Nature* exhibition, I planned four workshops with more than 200 available places, coordinated academic contributors, and converted specialist topics into accessible activity formats. The exhibition ran for one month at the university library and was later invited to Ningbo Library.

For a youth climate-action hackathon, I led a sponge-city exhibition module, developed its visual narrative and presentation structure, and contributed to a third-prize result.

## 🧭 Transferable operating framework

1. Identify the audience and the need behind their stated interest.
2. Select a theme and format with a clear reason to participate.
3. Coordinate content, promotion, logistics, and ownership.
4. Monitor registration and live feedback.
5. Review results and document what should change next time.

## 📈 What I would measure next

These projects pre-dated a formal analytics plan. If repeating them, I would track registration source, conversion from views to sign-ups, attendance rate, participation depth, satisfaction, and repeat attendance. This is a limitation of the original projects and an important lesson for future operations work.
